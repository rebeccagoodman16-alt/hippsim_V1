CA1 pyramidal cell models

One, two, or thousands of compartments

The CA1 models in Golomb 2006, Lowet 2023 and Ceballos 2025 are not simplified copies of a reconstructed neuron. They have one or two compartments: the soma, and sometimes one box that stands for “the dendrites”. The neurons on hippsim’s Neurons page are the traced cell itself, cut into hundreds or thousands of compartments. This page explains the difference, what each kind of model can tell you, and how the three papers lead to the full-morphology model.

A compartment is one patch of membrane with one voltage

Inside one compartment the voltage is the same everywhere. Between compartments current flows through a resistance, so neighbouring compartments can have different voltages. The more compartments, the more places the cell can be “at different voltages at the same time”. Below is the same CA1 pyramidal cell (n123, … traced points) as each kind of model sees it.

1 compartmentThe tree is ignored: the soma’s channels and the whole cell’s membrane act as one sphere. Input injected “into the cell” reaches every channel at once.
2 compartmentsA soma and one dendrite box joined by a coupling conductance g_c. The box has its own channels (NaP and slow K⁺ in Lowet; Iₕ in Ceballos). Its size and coupling are fitted, not measured from a tracing.
ReconstructionThe traced branches are cut into ≈ 1 500 pieces of ≤ 20 µm, each with its own voltage and channels, joined by the cytoplasm’s resistance. This is what the Neurons page simulates.

Neither is “more correct”. A small model isolates a mechanism and can be explored completely; a reconstruction adds the cell’s real geometry, at the cost of many more parameters that each need a source.

The three papers, in order

Each builds on the one before: Golomb gives the somatic burst currents, Lowet links bursts and single spikes to θ, γ and place fields with a two-compartment cell, Ceballos explains how one cell can carry both codes.

2006 · 1 compartment

Persistent Na⁺ and M-type K⁺ currents and somatic bursting

Golomb D, Yue C, Yaari Y (2006) J Neurophysiol 96:1912–1926 · doi · ModelDB 66268

Question
Which currents let a CA1 pyramidal cell fire bursts instead of single spikes?
Model and data
A one-compartment conductance-based CA1 pyramidal cell: transient and persistent Na⁺ (I_NaT, I_NaP), delayed-rectifier and A-type K⁺, and the M-current (I_M). Combined with recordings.
What they found
  • Increasing I_NaP induces bursting and adds spikes per burst, but it is neither necessary nor sufficient for bursting.
  • I_M enables bursting: its slow activation switches the cell between silent and tonically firing (“square bursting”, with I_M as the slow variable).
  • Lowering extracellular Ca²⁺ is modelled as shifting I_NaP activation to more negative voltages.
  • Different parameter sets reproduce the range of firing patterns seen in the recordings.
For place fields
It has no dendrite and no space, so it says nothing about place fields directly. It gives the somatic currents (I_NaP, I_M) that the later models build on.
In hippsim
The soma equations in hippsim’s Python ladder (ir_ladder) reproduce the spike times of Golomb 2006 Fig 6Da exactly (67 spikes; validate.py). The same currents sit at the soma and axon of the full-morphology research model.

2023 · 2 compartments

Theta and gamma rhythmic coding through two spike output modes

Lowet E, et al. (2023) Cell Reports 42:112906 · doi · code: HanLabBU/Lowet-Cell-Reports-2023

Question
Do CA1 cells send different messages with bursts and with single spikes during navigation?
Model and data
Recordings: tetrodes in rats on a 180 cm track (147 cells), SomArchon voltage imaging in head-fixed mice (56 cells), Allen Neuropixels data (1 689 cells). Model: a minimal two-compartment cell: soma with Na⁺/K⁺, dendrite with persistent Na⁺ and a slow K⁺ current (Pinsky–Rinzel style), driven by dendritic θ + γ input.
What they found
  • Bursts lock to θ; single spikes lock more to γ (strongest around mid-γ).
  • Bursts carry more spatial information, come earlier in the place field and precess more strongly with position; single spikes dominate as the animal leaves the field.
  • 8 Hz optogenetic pulses increase bursts; 40 Hz stimulation selectively increases single spikes; steady depolarization increases single spikes, not bursts.
  • In the model, a ramping (place-field-like) input reproduces the early-field bursts and their stronger precession.
For place fields
The first of the three to connect the two spike codes to place fields: bursts mark entering the field, single spikes leaving it.
In hippsim
The minimal model (dXdT_HH_3.m equations) is the default cell of the Place fields page. Its figures have not been re-run in hippsim for a numerical check.

2025 · 1 compartment, then 2 (soma + Iₕ dendrite)

Interleaved single and bursting spiking resonance

Ceballos CC, Chadly N, Lowet E, Pena RFO (2025) PLoS Comput Biol 21:e1013126 · doi · code: rodrigo-pena-lab/interleaved_resonance

Question
Do bursts and single spikes prefer different input rhythms, and which conductances decide?
Model and data
A CA1 soma derived from Golomb 2006 and Lowet 2023 (NaT, NaP, K_dr, M, A-type, high-threshold Ca²⁺, two Ca²⁺-activated K⁺ currents), refitted to voltage imaging; later a dendritic compartment with Iₕ. Inputs: sinusoidal currents at θ (10 Hz) and γ (40 Hz). A burst is spikes < 14 ms apart.
What they found
  • More g_NaP moves firing from single spikes to bursts, then to depolarization block; more g_Kdr moves it back to single spikes.
  • Single spikes and bursts each have a resonance, at different frequencies (“interleaved”); low g_NaP or high g_Kdr lock bursts to θ; g_h mainly affects γ.
  • Bursts follow long silences (> 100 ms); single spikes also follow short ones. With θ + γ together, the cell behaves as with θ alone.
  • Place fields are not simulated; the paper links its result to Lowet 2023 (bursts/θ entering a field, single spikes/γ leaving).
For place fields
It explains why one cell can carry both codes at once: the same voltage trace has a θ-preferring burst resonance and a different single-spike resonance.
In hippsim
Rebecca’s reproduction (Model ladder; Python ir_ladder): reproduced the interleaving (bursts 8–20 Hz, single spikes in γ), f_SS > f_BS for every g_h, and bursting from g_NaP ≈ 0.35. Not reproduced: the sign reversals of Δf with g_NaP and g_Kdr, and ΔA < 0. Uses the “hybrid” reading of the printed parameters (θ_p −35 mV).

From 1 compartment to the full cell: the research ladder

1 · Golomb 20061 compartment. Somatic I_NaP + I_M make bursts. Model ladder
2 · Lowet 20232 compartments. Dendritic NaP + slow K⁺; bursts at θ, single spikes at γ, place fields. Place fields
3 · Ceballos 2025Golomb-type soma + Iₕ dendrite. Interleaved burst / single-spike resonance. Resonance analysis
4 · Full morphologyThe same somatic I_M and I_NaP at the soma and axon of a reconstructed CA1 cell, with Migliore-derived dendrites (Iₕ and A-type gradients). A lab model, not yet validated. Open on n123

Going up a rung changes more than detail: the reconstruction’s dendrites draw current from the soma (lower input resistance), so conductances that made bursts in one compartment may not on the full tree, and must be re-checked, not copied.

Which should I use?

Use 1 or 2 compartments when the question is about which currents produce a firing pattern (bursts, resonance, phase locking), you need to sweep parameters, or you want many cells in a network.
Use a reconstruction when the question is about where: where a synapse sits, how input from entorhinal cortex vs CA3 reaches the soma, how far a spike back-propagates, or whether cell shape changes the answer.

Coming from the wet lab: a whole-cell recording at the soma sees something close to the 1-compartment picture when the cell is compact; dendritic recordings, local drug puffs and input-specific stimulation are where the reconstruction matters.
