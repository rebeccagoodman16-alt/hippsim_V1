What hippsim is

hippsim is a browser platform for simulating neurons, from one reconstructed cell to a network, with every baseline value, manipulation and source on record. It runs published models with its own in-browser solver and cross-checks that solver against NEURON and Brian2.

Layer What it is New?
Equations Cable equation + published channel and network models (Hodgkin–Huxley 1952, Migliore 2005, Golomb 2006, Brunel 2000) No: all from the literature
Solver hs-cable.js and the NGC engine: implicit compartmental solvers that run in the browser New implementation of standard methods
Platform 87 real cells in 3D, any model on any cell, baselines with sources, drug manipulations, saved runs, NEURON / Brian2 scripts, cross-engine checks Yes
Research model Golomb I_M + I_NaP on Migliore 2005 dendrites, θ + γ drive Lab model built from published parts

In one sentence: a browser-based platform for simulating hippocampal neurons on reconstructed morphologies, with an in-browser compartmental solver checked against NEURON and Brian2. It is not a replacement for NEURON, which is far more general; it makes published models on real cells quick to run, compare and record.

Quick start

From opening hippsim to a saved, citable run: six steps, nothing to install.

  1. Neurons (home page): pick a region or type a cell name (CA1, granule, n123).
  2. The cell opens in 3D. Drag to rotate, scroll or pinch to zoom, hover or tap a branch for its compartment and distance from the soma. Details shows lengths, diameters, branching and a Sholl profile.
  3. Press Simulate. Choose the scale (single cell, circuit, population), the model and where it runs (hippsim, NEURON, Brian2).
  4. Run baseline. Read the trace, the result and the baseline values with their sources; the voltage plays across the cell.
  5. Manipulate the baseline: a drug button (TTX, TEA, 4-AP, ZD7288, XE991, bicuculline, APV, NBQX), a slider, or tap a branch to move the electrode. Run: the new trace is drawn over the baseline.
  6. Save run. It appears in Runs and reopens into the same cell, model and settings.

Keyboard in the 3D view: space = rotate, 0 = front view, Esc = back to all neurons. Display (top right): text size, colours (incl. high contrast, colour-blind safe), light or dark, web or phone view.

The Simulate panel

Simulate opens beside the 3D cell: choose the scale, the model and the test, run the baseline, then manipulate anything. Nothing opens another page.

1. Choose

  • Scale: Single cell (this reconstruction), Circuit (this cell receiving synaptic input), Population (a network of point neurons).
  • Model: the models available for this cell (Models). The research model is offered on CA1 pyramidal cells only.
  • Test: the experiment to run. Single-cell models offer up to eight:
Test What it measures
Current step Firing pattern, first-spike latency, rate, adaptation
F–I curve Rate vs current: rheobase and gain
Iₕ sag and input resistance Input resistance, sag ratio, rebound
Back-propagating spike Peak depolarization vs distance from the soma; the cell coloured by peak voltage
Clustered tuft input Distal AMPA + NMDA synapses: dendritic vs somatic response
Scattered dendritic input Synapses over the whole tree
Theta-burst 4 × 4 pulses: how many fire a spike
Resonance (ZAP) Impedance vs frequency, resonance frequency, Q
θ + γ drive (research model) Single spikes vs bursts, with the drive at the soma, radiatum or tuft
  • Cards show a short title and the essentials (source, channels, temperature). A model whose parameters come from CA1 pyramidal cells carries a warning on any other cell, e.g. CA1-derived parameters on a CA3 pyramidal cell: not validated for CA3. Details under the tests holds the full description.
  • Run in: for the Hodgkin–Huxley current step, hippsim, NEURON or Brian2. Everything else runs in hippsim; the panel names the original simulator.

2. Baseline

Run baseline runs the model and test with their documented values. You get the plot (voltage traces, an x–y curve, or a raster), a one-line result, a readout table and Where this comes from (below). The voltage (or peak voltage) plays across the 3D cell.

Where this comes from

Five rows, each opening a panel, keep apart what is measured and what is modelled:

Row What it shows
Experimental morphology Cell identity, source archive, reconstruction ID, original and source papers, how it was traced; then what hippsim estimated or changed (trunk / obliques / tuft, soma as a sphere, soma–dendrite junction, zero-length points, axon stub)
Published model Each mechanism with its model source and original reference, passive values with their sources, and the channel distribution across soma, AIS, axon, basal dendrites, apical trunk, obliques and tuft. Anything without a known source says unspecified
Adapted here How the model was applied to this cell (e.g. a CA1-derived parameterization on a CA3 morphology) and your manipulations
Tested computationally The test, the simulator, every value in this run, and the assumptions (temperature, Q10, time step, discretization, initial state)
Validated What has been checked (same model in NEURON and Brian2, Neuron-lab checks) and what has not (recordings). For any cell: Compare hippsim and NEURON in detail overlays the two simulators' traces at the soma, AIS and dendrites with the full metric, set-up and morphology tables (Validation)

Explanations on hover

Display → Show explanations on hover (on by default). When on, pointing at (or tapping) a model, test, channel, slider, branch tool or part of the cell shows what it is: biological function for channels, anatomical and electrophysiological role for compartments, what is measured for tests. When off, nothing pops up; cards stay short either way, and Details and the rows above keep the full information.

3. Manipulate

Manipulate the baseline opens everything, grouped: - Published manipulations: drug and model presets (TTX, TEA, 4-AP, ZD7288, XE991, Aβ-like A-type reduction, passive dendrites, uniform densities, room temperature, …). Hover for the source (with explanations on); press again to undo. - Branch tools: pick one, then tap a branch: Electrode (where current goes), Probe (record there), Synapse (AMPA + NMDA at 10 ms), Local TTX, Block A-type, Block Iₕ, Make passive, Cut. Blocks apply from that point outward. Each mark is listed and can be removed. - Grouped sliders: Stimulus (the test's own settings), Channels, Where channels are (soma, basal, apical, axon), Gradients, Passive (R_m, R_a, C_m, rest, spines), Temperature. Each group shows how many values changed.

Run draws the new result over the baseline; the readout shows both columns, with changed values highlighted. back to baseline resets everything.

4. Save

Save run stores the cell, model, test, simulator, every value, every branch mark, the changes from baseline, the readout and the plot. See Saved runs and scripts.

The 3D view while simulating

Compartment toggles, colour by compartment, distance, voltage (played over time) or peak V, Details (beside the panel on large screens), screenshot and video all stay available. Cut branches turn grey.

Equations and numerics

Every single-cell and circuit model solves the compartmental cable equation on the reconstruction. For compartment i with neighbours j:

\[C_i \frac{dV_i}{dt} = -\sum_{\mathrm{ion}} g_{\mathrm{ion},i}(V_i,t)\,(V_i - E_{\mathrm{ion}}) - g_{L,i}(V_i - E_L) + \sum_{j} g_{ij}\,(V_j - V_i) + I_{\mathrm{inj},i}(t) + I_{\mathrm{syn},i}(t)\]

Channel conductances follow gating variables; for Hodgkin–Huxley Na⁺ and K⁺:

\[g_{\mathrm{Na}} = \bar g_{\mathrm{Na}}\, m^3 h, \qquad g_{\mathrm{K}} = \bar g_{\mathrm{K}}\, n^4, \qquad \frac{dx}{dt} = \frac{x_\infty(V) - x}{\tau_x(V)}, \quad x \in \{m, h, n\}\]

The population model is a leaky integrate-and-fire network (Brunel 2000):

\[\tau \frac{dV_k}{dt} = -V_k + R I_k(t), \qquad V_k > \theta \Rightarrow \text{spike},\; V_k \leftarrow V_r \text{ for } \tau_{rp}\]
Step Method
Discretization Hodgkin–Huxley (hs-cable.js): compartments ≤ 20 µm along unbranched paths; soma = one compartment with sphere area. Migliore, research, circuit (NGC): 20–25 µm compartments; a 200 µm axon stub when no axon is traced
Voltage Backward Euler, solved exactly on the tree by Hines elimination (O(N) per step), as NEURON's default
Gates Exponential Euler with rate tables (−120 to +80 mV)
Time step 0.025 ms (HH, Migliore), 0.05 ms (research model, circuit), 0.1 ms (population, exact LIF decay)
Spikes Soma crossing 0 mV, counted during the stimulus window
Synapses Double-exponential conductances; NMDA with Mg²⁺ block (Jahr & Stevens 1990)
Units mV, ms, nA, µS, nF, µm

The NGC engine (Migliore, research model, circuit) is copied unchanged from the lab's Little Neural Guide at build time (site/hs-ngc.js), so the Simulate panel and the Neuron lab run identical code.

Validation

hippsim's Hodgkin–Huxley solver gives NEURON's spike count exactly on 51 of 87 cells and within one spike on 74; Brian2 matches on 65 and 81. Every cell was run in all three engines with the same model (0.5 nA, 300 ms step at the soma) by tools/engine_check.py.

Comparison (spike counts) Same count Within ±1 spike
hippsim vs NEURON 9.0.2 51 / 87 74 / 87
hippsim vs Brian2 2.11 65 / 87 81 / 87

The gaps come from discretization: hippsim ≤ 20 µm compartments, NEURON d_lambda 0.1, Brian2 one compartment per traced segment. They are largest in cells near depolarization block, where a small difference in input resistance decides between repetitive firing and a single spike.

Detailed comparison: hippsim vs NEURON

Spike counts alone hide why two simulators differ. tools/compare_engines.py runs the same Hodgkin–Huxley model on every cell in NEURON 9 and in hippsim's engine and records, for each:

  • voltage at the soma, the axon initial segment (axon point nearest 30 µm) and apical (or basal) dendrites at ~100 and ~300 µm;
  • resting potential, peak, spike count, first-spike latency, threshold (where dV/dt rises above 10 mV/ms before the first spike) and maximum dV/dt;
  • soma Na⁺ and K⁺ currents;
  • membrane area per compartment type, number of compartments, passive input resistance and time constant (−0.05 nA, channels off);
  • the set-up: equations, conductance densities, units, reversal potentials, temperature, stimulus, initial conditions, time step and integration method, each marked same or different;
  • the first time the soma traces are more than 1 mV apart.

Then it changes one thing at a time in either simulator (finer compartments, NEURON's soma area, NEURON's soma–dendrite junction, the cleaned SWC, NEURON's own geometry after Import3d) and reports the spike count of each. Nothing is tuned: the published / default parameters are kept and the differences are documented. Open any cell, Simulate → Validated → Compare hippsim and NEURON in detail to see the traces overlaid.

Worked example: CA3 pyramidal cell l24b

hippsim fires no spike for the 0.5 nA step; NEURON fires one (10.6 ms after onset). Equations, densities, reversal potentials, temperature, stimulus, initial state and time step are the same, and the soma area is identical (2 030.6 µm²). The difference is geometric:

  • the reconstruction has 7 branches attached to the soma, and one of them starts inside the soma;
  • hippsim's cable model starts each soma-attached branch at the soma centre, so it adds membrane between the centre and the branch's first point and keeps the part of a dendrite that lies inside the soma; NEURON's Import3d starts the branch at its first point and trims it;
  • hippsim's basal membrane is 35 208 µm² against NEURON's 31 072 µm², and its passive input resistance is 31.4 MΩ against 34.1 MΩ (8 % lower);
  • this cell sits just at threshold, so 8 % less input resistance is enough to stop the spike.

How to cite

Cite hippsim and the models and cells you used; every saved run's zip carries the references for its model and cell.

  • hippsim: CITATION.cff (version 0.12). A Zenodo release will add a DOI once the repository is public.
  • Models: Hodgkin & Huxley 1952, J Physiol 117:500 · Migliore, Ferrante & Ascoli 2005, J Neurophysiol 94:4145 (ModelDB 55035) · Golomb, Yue & Yaari 2006, J Neurophysiol 96:1912 · Brunel 2000, J Comput Neurosci 8:183 · each tool's sources on Models.
  • Cells: the paper on each cell's page (morphologies/references.bib); for NeuroMorpho.Org cells also Ascoli et al. 2007, J Neurosci 27:9247.
  • Simulators used for cross-checks: NEURON (Hines & Carnevale 1997, Neural Comput 9:1179); Brian2 (Stimberg, Brette & Goodman 2019, eLife 8:e47314).

Methods text you can adapt:

Simulations were run in hippsim (v0.12), a browser-based platform that solves the compartmental cable equation on reconstructed morphologies (backward Euler with Hines elimination, dt 0.025 ms). Its spike counts were cross-checked against NEURON 9.0.2 and Brian2 2.11 on 87 cells.

Limitations

hippsim is for exploring, teaching and comparing published models on real cells; its results are model predictions, not fits to recordings.

  • The Hodgkin–Huxley model uses one channel density for every cell, chosen so typical cells fire; it is not cell-type specific.
  • NEURON and Brian2 results in the app are precomputed baselines; manipulations run in hippsim, with a script to rerun them elsewhere.
  • The Migliore and research models were written for CA1 pyramidal cells; on other cell types they are a what-if.
  • Circuit input numbers and rates are model choices. The population is a scaled Brunel network, so the boundaries between his regimes shift.
  • NEURON's extended impedance with Iₕ disagrees with the reference engine (known issue, see Validation).
